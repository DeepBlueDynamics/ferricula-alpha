## 1. Executive Summary & Doctrinal Landscape

In classical Mahāyāna and Tibetan epistemological debates, the most severe, politically explosive, and technically demanding schism concerns the interpretation of **Śūnyatā** (Emptiness) and the **Two Truths** (*Satyadvaya*): the dispute between **Rangtong** (*rang stong*, "self-emptiness") and **Shentong** (*gzhan stong*, "other-emptiness").

- **Rangtong (Mainstream Gelug / Prāsaṅgika-Mādhyamaka):** Formulated rigorously by Je Tsongkhapa (1357–1419). Emptiness is an absolute, non-affirming negation (*prasajya-pratiṣedha*). Every conceivable dharma—from a clattering cup to the omniscient mind of a Buddha—is dependently arisen (*pratītyasamutpāda*) and strictly devoid of inherent existence (*svabhāva*). There is no metaphysical substratum, no transcendental foundation, and no privileged ontological base. Emptiness itself is empty (*śūnyatā-śūnyatā*).
- **Shentong (Jonang / Great Mādhyamaka / Dölpopa Sherab Gyaltsen):** Formulated systematically in the 14th century by Dölpopa Sherab Gyaltsen (1292–1361) in his magnum opus *Ri chos nges don rgya mtsho* (*Mountain Doctrine: Ocean of Definitive Meaning*). Dölpopa posits a radical asymmetry between the Two Truths:
  1. **Relative Reality (*saṃvṛti-satya*):** All conditioned, impermanent, dependently arisen phenomena—including discursive thought, emotional afflictions, and the eighth consciousness itself (*ālayavijñāna* / *kun gzhi rnam shes*)—are **Rangtong** (empty of their own nature, lacking inherent existence, unfindable under analytical deconstruction).
  2. **Ultimate Reality (*paramārtha-satya*):** The unconditioned, immutable ground of reality—the Buddha-nature (*tathāgatagarbha* / *buddhadhātu*), Dharmadhātu, and primordial gnosis of the ground-of-all (*ālaya-jñāna* / *kun gzhi ye shes*)—is **Shentong** (empty of *other*, adventitious defilements and relative fabrications), but **not empty of its own radiant, indestructible, eternal enlightened attributes (*buddhaguṇa*)**.

This monograph examines the historical suppression and philosophical anatomy of Shentong as an **ontological outlier**, contrasts the seven classical taxonomies of other-emptiness, and details how the Shentong-Rangtong duality directly provides the missing formal foundation for dual-plane storage invariants in autonomous cognitive architectures (such as Ferricula/LUME).

## 2. The Doctrinal Civil War: Rangtong vs. Shentong

### 2.1 The Rangtong Invariant: Radical Nominalism and Non-Affirming Negation

For Tsongkhapa and the Prāsaṅgika orthodoxy, the realization of emptiness consists entirely in the deconstruction of *svabhāva* (intrinsic reality). The ultimate truth is neither an entity, a consciousness, nor an ontological field. As Susan Kahn observes:
> *"Ultimate truth does not point to a transcendent reality, but to the transcendence of deception. It is critical to emphasize that the ultimate truth of emptiness is a negational truth... just as a room without an elephant in it does not contain an elephantless substance."*

Under Rangtong:
$$\forall x, \quad \text{Dharma}(x) \implies \text{Svabhāva-śūnya}(x)$$
$$\text{Paramārtha-satya} \equiv \neg \text{Svabhāva} \quad (\text{Prasajya-pratiṣedha})$$

If an ultimate reality existed with its own unconditioned self-nature (*svabhāva*), it would violate the universal law of dependent arising (*pratītyasamutpāda*). Hence, any philosophy asserting an inherently existent Buddha-wisdom is denounced by Rangtong scholars as a relapse into the *ātman* of non-Buddhist Indian philosophies (Vedānta/Sāṃkhya)—the fatal charge of being **"Crypto-Vedāntists"** (*rgya gar gyi mu stegs pa*).

### 2.2 The Shentong Revolt: Radical Asymmetry and Definitive Meaning

Dölpopa Sherab Gyaltsen rejected the universal application of self-emptiness as an incomplete, provisional teaching (*neyārtha*) belonging exclusively to the Second Turning of the Wheel of Dharma (the Prajñāpāramitā corpus). Drawing upon the Third Turning sūtras (*Tathāgatagarbha Sūtra*, *Śrīmālādevī*, *Mahāyāna Mahāparinirvāṇa*, *Saṃdhinirmocana*) and the Indian *Maitreya-Asaṅga* treatises (especially the *Ratnagotravibhāga / Uttaratantra*), Dölpopa argued that universal negation leads to nihilistic collapse (*chad lta*) if not grounded in an unconditioned, self-luminous reality.

Dölpopa established the foundational distinction in *Mountain Doctrine*:
> *"Because all that is present as the two modes of emptiness are equal in being emptiness, there are statements with the single phrase, 'All is emptiness,' but there are also statements that distinguish between empty of self-nature and empty of other. Concerning that, because relative and incidental entities are completely nonexistent in their true mode of existence, they are empty of own-essence. That is being empty of self-nature. Because the original absolute that is empty of those relative phenomena is never nonexistent, it is empty of other."*

In Shentong formal notation:
$$\text{Relative}(x) \implies \text{Rangtong}(x) \iff \text{Svabhāva-śūnya}(x)$$
$$\text{Ultimate}(\Omega) \implies \text{Shentong}(\Omega) \iff (\Omega \text{ is empty of } x) \land \neg(\Omega \text{ is empty of } \Omega)$$

The ultimate ground $\Omega$ is endowed with perfections of Permanence (*nitya*), Purity (*śubha*), Bliss (*sukha*), and Self (*ātman* / *mahātman*), deliberately reintroducing classical terms that early Buddhism deconstructed, but re-anchoring them in non-dual primordial awareness (*jñāna* / *ye shes*).

## 3. Indian Ancestry and Scriptural Foundations

Shentong is not a 14th-century Tibetan fabrication; Dölpopa and Tāranātha traced it to well-documented Indian antecedents:

1. **The *Ratnagotravibhāga* (Uttaratantra I.157–158):**
   > *"The basic element is empty of what is adventitious, which has the characteristic of being separable. It is not empty of the unsurpassable attributes, which have the characteristic of being inseparable."*
   This verse is the locus classicus of Shentong logic: the ultimate substrate is separable from transient defilements (*āgantukamala*), but inseparable from enlightened qualities (*guṇa*).
2. **The *Bṛhaṭṭīkā* (Large Prajñāpāramitā Commentary):**
   Commentary attributed to Vasubandhu or Damṣṭrāsena on the Maitreya Chapter:
   > *"Being empty means being devoid of what is other (*pararahita* / *gzhan bral*)."*
   The perfected nature (*pariniṣpanna*) is explicitly stated to be empty of the dependent (*paratantra*) and imagined (*parikalpita*) natures.
3. **Indian Masters:**
   - **Ratnākaraśānti (c. 1000 CE):** Maintained the *Alīkākāravāda-Yogācāra* position wherein the perfected nature is pure, luminous, self-cognizing mind (*prakṛti-prabhāsvara-citta*) devoid of mental representations (*ākāra*).
   - **Sajjana & Ānandakīrti:** Authors of *Mahāyānottaratantraśāstropadeśa*, preserving the Maitreya transmission into Tibet via Ngog Lotsāwa.
   - **Kālacakra Tantric Lineage:** Kashmiri pandit Candranātha and Yumo Mikyö Dorje (11th century), teaching the visible reflections of emptiness (*śūnyatā-bimba*) in the Six-Branch Yoga (*ṣaḍaṅga-yoga*).

## 4. Typology: The Seven Forms of Shentong

As demonstrated by modern scholars (Karl Brunnhölzl, 2014) citing Surmang Padma Namgyal, Shentong is not a monolithic doctrine. Seven distinct variants emerged across Tibetan traditions:

| Lineage / Master | Formulational Distinction | Relative (Rangtong / Self-Empty) | Ultimate (Shentong / Other-Empty) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Jonang (Dölpopa)** | Ontological / Dual-Nature | Impermanent consciousness (*vijñāna* / *kun gzhi rnam shes*) | Unconditioned Buddha-wisdom (*jñāna* / *kun gzhi ye shes*) |
| **2. Śākya Chokden (Sakya)** | Epistemic Appearance vs. Essence | Phenomenal appearance (*snang ba*) | Natural luminosity (*'od gsal*) |
| **3. Sabsang Mati Paṇchen** | Dualistic Cleavage | Subject-object duality (*gzung 'dzin*) | Inseparable expanse and awareness (*dbyings rig zung 'jug*) |
| **4. Düdül Dorje (13th Karmapa)** | Cosmological / Soteriological | Saṃsāric conditioned reality | Nirvāṇic unconditioned reality |
| **5. Mikyö Dorje (8th Karmapa)** | Mode of Being vs. Manifestation | Self-empty in mode of existence (*gnas lugs*) | Other-empty in mode of appearance (*snang lugs*) |
| **6. Situ Panchen (8th Tai Situpa)** | Dialectical / Conceptual | Negative deconstructive analysis | Affirmative direct recognition |
| **7. Gedsé Paṇchen (Nyingma)** | Experiential Meditation Phases | Analytical resolve during meditative equipoise (*mnyam bzhag*) | Clear discrimination during post-equipoise (*rjes thob*) |

Brunnhölzl classifies these seven into three functional meta-categories:
- **Ontological Stratification (1–5):** Two distinct ontological levels of reality (relative appearance vs. ultimate substrate).
- **Dialectical Method (6):** Two conceptual approaches to investigating reality (reductive critique vs. ostensive designation).
- **Phenomenological Phases (7):** Distinct non-conceptual stages of meditative practice (radical deconstructive emptiness during insight vs. dynamic cognitive integration in post-meditation).

## 5. The Critical Outlier: *Kun-gzhi Rnam-shes* vs. *Kun-gzhi Ye-shes*

The pivotal doctrinal mechanism separating Shentong from orthodox Yogācāra and Gelug Mādhyamaka lies in the bifurcation of the eighth consciousness (**Ālayavijñāna**):

```
                        THE GROUND DUALITY (KUN GZHI)
                                      │
           ┌──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┐
           ▼                                                     ▼
   KUN GZHI RNAM SHES                                    KUN GZHI YE SHES
   (Ālayavijñāna - Defiled Storage)                      (Ālaya-jñāna - Primordial Wisdom)
           │                                                     │
   - Rangtong (Empty of Self-Nature)                    - Shentong (Empty of Other)
   - Impermanent, conditioned stream                    - Uncreated, immutable, permanent
   - Vehicle of karmic seeds (Bīja)                     - Endowed with 32 Buddha-qualities
   - Collapses at Enlightenment                         - The ultimate nature of Dharmakāya
   - Obscuring cloud covering the sky                  - The luminous sky itself
```

In standard Faxiang and Gelug formulations, *Ālayavijñāna* is purely defiled and conditioned; when enlightenment occurs, it undergoes revolution of the basis (*āśrayaparavṛtti*) to become the Great Mirror Wisdom (*ādarśa-jñāna*).

In Jonang Shentong, however, **Kun-gzhi Ye-shes** is not produced by purification; it was *never defiled to begin with*. It is the eternal, primordial ground. The defiled *Kun-gzhi Rnam-shes* is merely an adventitious perceptual error (*bhrānti*) that projects upon this primordial wisdom.

## 6. Political Suppression and Rimé Revival

The radical nature of Shentong triggered severe political and institutional consequences:
1. **The 1658 Gelug Edict:** Following the defeat of the King of Tsang (a Jonang patron) by the Mongol forces of Gushri Khan supporting the 5th Dalai Lama (Ngawang Lobsang Gyatso), the Jonang school was officially proscribed.
   - Jonang monasteries in Central Tibet (including Takten Damchö Ling) were seized and converted to the Gelug school.
   - Printing blocks for Dölpopa's and Tāranātha's texts were sealed under governmental authority or burned.
   - The teaching of Shentong was forbidden in central provinces, branded as heresy that compromised Mādhyamaka non-essentialism.
2. **Survival in the East:** Jonang survived intact in remote pockets of Amdo (Dzamtang, Golok) where Central Tibetan governmental authority could not reach.
3. **The 19th-Century Rimé Revival:** Masters of the non-sectarian (*Rimé*) movement—notably Katok Tsewang Norbu, Situ Panchen, Jamgön Kongtrül Lodrö Thayé, and Jamgön Ju Mipham Gyatso—revived Shentong texts. Kongtrül integrated Shentong into Kagyu Mahāmudrā curriculum, establishing it as the crown jewel of the Third Turning.

## 7. Architectural Translation: The Dual-Plane Storage Invariant

Why is the Shentong outlier essential for autonomous cognitive memory systems (Ferricula, LUME, and beyond)?

Autonomous agents operating in complex environments encounter a fatal architectural trade-off:
- **Pure Rangtong Failure Mode (Catastrophic Fluidity / Hallucinatory Drift):** If the memory store treats *all* records as dependently arisen hypotheses subject to continuous rewrites, merges, and updates, the system lacks an unconditioned epistemological ground. Under ambiguous inputs (such as sensory clutter or contradictory user prompts), the entire memory graph drifts. This is the AI equivalent of nihilism (*ucchedavāda*): no claim is verifiable because every node is relative to an editable context.
- **Pure Essentialist Failure Mode (Dogmatic Stagnation / Frozen Noise):** If the system treats transient sensory perceptions or initial inferences as immutable ground truth, false hypotheses (e.g. an unobserved "kick trajectory" or phantom "bookshelf") become permanently etched into the knowledge base, corrupting future queries.

### 7.1 The Two-Truths Memory Architecture

Jonang Shentong provides the precise formal reconciliation via **Asymmetric Two-Truths Storage**:

|                   SHENTONG PLANE (PARAMĀRTHA-SATYA)                       |
|           Cryptographically Immutable Prior / Verified Ground Truth       |

### 7.2 Core Architectural Invariants Derived from Shentong

1. **Dual-Plane Separation Invariant:**
   The base ground-truth ledger and the runtime hypothesis overlay must never share the same storage mutability contract. The base ledger must be mounted `:ro` (read-only) or secured by hash chains. It represents the *Shentong* ground: empty of runtime conjecture, unalterable by agent runtime writes.
2. **Negative Proof Invariance (*Nimitta-Śūnyatā*):**
   A negative observation (e.g., `NotSeenInScope` under the couch) belongs to the verified ground plane. It is not an absence of data; it is an affirmative record of a scoped negative test. The hypothesis overlay must not "fill in" the negative observation with speculative positive objects.
3. **Regress Termination via Mutual Verification (*Sì Fēn*):**
   Hypothesis expansion in the Rangtong plane is bounded by the Shentong base. Any proposed graph edge between a goal and an observation must trace directly to a verified sensory record in the base ledger, preventing unanchored associative drift (*dúyǐngjìng*).
