## 1. The Archetypal Monk-Engineer: Ācariya Anuruddha

When tracing who literally "built" the Abhidharma cognitive architecture, historical and technical inquiry converges on one pre-eminent figure: **Ācariya Anuruddha** (circa 11th–12th Century CE, Polonnaruwa, Sri Lanka).

### 1.1 Who He Was
- An ordained Theravāda Buddhist monk and elder residing at the Mūlasoma Vihāra in Polonnaruwa, Sri Lanka.
- The author of the ***Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha*** (The Compendium of Things Contained in the Abhidhamma), a 50-page manual that accomplished what 40+ volumes of earlier canonical texts could not: an **executable, modular system specification of the human mind**.

### 1.2 What He Built
1. **The 17-Moment Citta-Vīthi State Machine (Chapter IV):**
   Anuruddha took disparate sutta references to perceptual sequences and formalized them into a strict **17-moment discrete-time state transition machine**. He mapped the exact progression from background equilibrium (*Atīta-bhavaṅga*), through sensation, reception, and investigation, to seven active transformation moments (*Javana*), terminating in two-stage registration (*Tadālambana*).
2. **The 52-Cetasika Combinatorial Matrix (Chapter II):**
   He categorized mental factors (*cetasikas*) into a precise object-oriented hierarchy:
   - 7 Universal Factors (*sabbacittasādhāraṇa*): Contact, Feeling, Perception, Volition, One-pointedness, Life-faculty, Attention.
   - 6 Occasionals (*pakiṇṇaka*).
   - 14 Unwholesome Factors (*akusala*): Greed, Hatred, Delusion, Perplexity (*vicikicchā*), etc.
   - 25 Beautiful Factors (*sobhana*): Mindfulness (*sati*), Wisdom (*paññā*), Equanimity (*tatra-majjhattatā*).
3. **The Concept of the Life-Continuum / Background Process (*Bhavaṅga*):**
   In Chapter V (*Vīthimutta-saṅgaha*), Anuruddha modeled the baseline background stream of consciousness that runs during deep sleep and in the pauses between active sensory events—the exact computational analog of Ferricula's idle clock thread and reservoir accumulation.

## 2. The Modern Ordained Monks in AI and Computing

### 2.1 Shinzen Young (Ordained Shingon Monk)
- **Background:** An American contemplative scholar formally ordained as a Buddhist monk in Japan at Mount Kōya in the Shingon Vajrayana tradition.
- **Contribution:** Known as the father of "Algorithmic Meditation." Shinzen developed **Unified Mindfulness**, a mathematical, process-oriented framework designed to deconstruct conscious experience into sensory elements (See In, See Out, Hear In, Hear Out, Feel In, Feel Out).
- **SEMA Lab (University of Arizona):** Founded the Sonication of Enhanced Mind Awareness (SEMA) Lab, utilizing transcranial ultrasound (TUS) and EEG neuromodulation to modulate the Default Mode Network (DMN), treating the mind explicitly as a computational network subject to signal-to-noise optimization.

### 2.2 Ven. Prof. Seiji Kumagai (Ordained Tendai Monk & AI Roboticist)
- **Background:** Professor at Kyoto University’s Institute for the Future of Human Society and an ordained monk in the Tendai school.
- **Contribution:** Led the creation of **BuddhaBot**, **BuddhaBot-Plus**, and **Buddharoid**—a physical humanoid robot monk powered by an AI system trained on Sanskrit and Pali canonical scriptures. The project models doctrinal retrieval to address clergy shortages in Japan, serving as a live hardware deployment of Buddhist wisdom retrieval.

### 2.3 Ven. Yuttadhammo Bhikkhu (Theravāda Monk & Open-Source Developer)
- **Background:** Canadian-born Buddhist monk ordained in Thailand, practicing in the lineage of Mahasi Sayadaw.
- **Contribution:** Developed the **Digital Pali Reader (DPR)**, open-source desktop and mobile meditation logging systems, and cross-platform Pali lookup tools. He pioneered the intersection of strict Theravāda monastic discipline with open-source software engineering.
