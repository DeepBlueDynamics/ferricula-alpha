### 2.1 The Doctrinal Anomaly
In Chapter 3 of the *Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra*, the Buddha makes an astonishing admission regarding the pedagogy of the Three Turnings:
* **The First Turning (Śrāvakayāna / Nikāyas):** Taught only **Six Consciousnesses** (the five senses and mental consciousness). The storehouse consciousness was intentionally concealed.
* **The Reason for Concealment:** The continuous, unbroken stream of latent seeds (*citta-santāna*) flows so smoothly and continuously that ordinary minds (*bāla*) cannot distinguish it from a permanent, unchanging metaphysical Soul (*ātman*).
* **The Cognitive Paradox:** 
  * If you reveal the storehouse, the mind reifies it into an ego/soul, inflating defensive self-attachment (*ātma-sneha*).
  * If you conceal the storehouse, the mind cannot explain memory continuity, karmic retribution across sleep/coma, or the survival of seeds in thoughtless states (*nirodhasamāpatti*).

### 3.1 The Hidden History
* While standard histories depict Xuanzang’s translation bureau as a unified monolith, it was fractured by an intense intellectual civil war between his two leading disciples:
  1. **Kuiji (窺基):** Wealthy aristocrat of the Yuwen clan. He enforced strict Faxiang orthodoxy: exactly eight consciousnesses, hardwired *Icchantika* exclusion, and total rejection of Paramārtha's 9th consciousness.
  2. **Woncheuk (圓測):** Brilliant Korean scholar-monk from Silla. Woncheuk recognized that Kuiji’s rigid eight-consciousness model severed Yogācāra from universal Buddha-nature. He wrote the *Chéng Wéishì Lùn Cèshū*, incorporating Paramārtha’s 9th consciousness (*Amalavijñāna*) and the *Ekayāna* (universal salvation).
* **The Sectarian Sabotage:** Kuiji’s disciples accused Woncheuk of bribery and intellectual theft, attempting to excise his commentaries from the imperial canon.
* **The Tibetan Inversion:** Despite Chinese marginalization, Woncheuk’s massive commentary on the *Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra* (*Haesimmilgyeongso*, 解深密經疏) was translated into Tibetan in the 9th century by Chokro Lüi Gyaltsen. When **Je Tsongkhapa** (founder of the Gelug school) wrote his masterwork *Legs bshad snying po*, he crowned Woncheuk's work as **"The Great Chinese Commentary"**, relying on Woncheuk rather than Kuiji!

  * **Kuiji’s Model (Closed Orthodoxy):** Strict 8-layer closed system with immutable limits (*Icchantika*).
  * **Woncheuk’s Model (Open Synthesis):** Layered system with an unconditioned, immutable ground tier (*Amalavijñāna*) enabling universal self-correction and continuous domain transfer.
